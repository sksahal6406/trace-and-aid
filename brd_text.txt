Executive Summary TVS Auto Assist operates a roadside assistance (RSA) business serving customers whose vehicles break down on the road. Today, the customer journey is heavily call-driven and manually orchestrated — customers phone in, L1 agents capture details, L2 agents hunt for available drivers or technicians through repeated calls, and field movement is tracked through more calls. The result is high TAT, low agent productivity, poor customer experience, and limited visibility for management. Phase 1 of the TVS Auto Assist transformation addresses these gaps through four integrated workstreams: A WhatsApp Bot that delivers a human-less customer journey for the three highest-volume flows — Buy RSA, Renew, and Claim Service — completing case creation, communication, vendor tracking, and CSAT entirely on WhatsApp. A revamped CRM for L2 agents with real-time GPS tracking, actionable queues, time-lapse vs SLA indicators, and role-based dashboards for RNMs, ZMs, and management. A smart Vendor Assignment Page that surfaces the nearest available vendor with distance, ETA, and colour -coded availability, eliminating manual hunting. And the TVS Buddy App 2.0 for field drivers and technicians, introducing bidding, auto-assignment, selfie-based attendance, real-time incentive withdrawal, automated billing, and multi-bucket TAT tracking. 4. Business Context &amp; Problem Statement TVS Auto Assist provides roadside assistance to vehicle owners across India through a network of company-owned operations (COCO) and Authorized Service Providers (ASPs). The service covers two primary intervention types: Repair-on-Site (ROS) by a technician for fixable issues such as flat tyres , dead batteries, or minor mechanical problems, and Towing to a workshop or drop location for cases that cannot be resolved on the spot. The business currently runs on a layered support model. An L1 customer care agent answers the inbound call, authenticates the customer, captures incident details and location, and creates a service ticket. The ticket then moves to an L2 agent, who is responsible for identifying the right field resource — a driver for towing or a technician for ROS — and assigning the case. ASP technicians and company drivers operate in parallel, and the L2 agent today has limited visibility into who is available, where they are, and how long they will take to reach the customer. This model worked at lower volumes but does not scale. As customer expectations rise and operational volumes grow, the manual, call-mediated nature of the workflow has become the dominant constraint on TAT, agent productivity, and customer satisfaction. The client has therefore prioritized Phase 1 to digitize the customer intake (WhatsApp), instrument the field operation (GPS, app discipline), and equip the agent and management layers with real-time visibility and actionable controls. 4. 1 Key Problems # Problem Title Description 1 No real-time availability tracking L2 agents cannot see which drivers or technicians are free, busy, in transit, or not logged into the app. 2 No live location tracking post-assignment Once a case is assigned, agents have no visibility into journey status, current location, or ETA without calling the field staff. 3 Excessive call volume between agents and field staff Agents repeatedly call drivers and technicians to check availability, journey start, and progress, consuming time meant for case management. 4 Manual, inefficient technician discovery Identifying the nearest available and qualified resource is done manually with no system-driven recommendation or auto-assignment logic. 5 Heavy human dependency in customer intake Every case — including routine renewals and RSA purchases — flows through an L1 agent, with no self-service WhatsApp or digital channel. 6 Fragmented communication across channels Customer, agent, driver, and technician communication is scattered across calls, SMS, and ad-hoc messages with no unified thread. 7 No proactive SLA / TAT intervention Agents lack real-time time-lapse vs SLA indicators and actionable queues to flag stalled journeys, mid-route stops, or impending breaches. 8 Limited visibility for network &amp; Business Requirements 7 .1 WhatsApp Bot – Self- Service Claim Journey Business need : Eliminate human dependency in routine customer intake and provide a digital-first channel for customers who prefer self-service over calling. 1. Entry Customer messages to the official WhatsApp number (via "Hi", QR, or deep link). Bot replies with a welcome message and a single tappable CTA: Claim . 2. Authentication Bot asks for one of: registered mobile, vehicle number, or policy/certificate number. System validates against the customer master via API. Success → greets by name, confirms vehicle on record. Failure → 2 retries, then live-agent handoff. 3. Probing Bot runs a configured question sequence — mostly tap-to-select (radio/quick-reply), free text only where needed. System validates each input, stores answers against the in-progress SR, and holds session state so the customer can resume after short pauses. 4. Location Capture Bot requests the customer's location via WhatsApp's native location share. System parses lat /long and reverse-geocodes to a readable address. Fallback: manual address entry if the customer declines or fails. 5. SR Creation &amp; Triage With auth + probing + location captured, system creates the SR in the CRM via API, generates a reference number, and tags it with conversation ID, customer ID, vehicle ID, answers, location, and channel = "WhatsApp Bot". A rule-based triage layer classifies it as Towing or ROS before handoff. 6. Confirmation &amp; Handoff Customer receives instant WhatsApp confirmation with the SR number and the next-step message. Case lands in the L2 queue, pre-classified and fully tagged. Exception Handling Drop-off → reminder after a configurable timeout. Auth fails twice → live-agent handoff. Long pause → fresh session; short pause → resume. Every inbound/outbound message logged against the SR for audit. Auto-classify every claim SR as Towing or ROS (Repair-on-Site) before it lands in the L2 queue, based on the customer's probing answers on WhatsApp. 1. Trigger Triage runs automatically once the customer completes the probing sequence in the WhatsApp Claim flow — before SR creation is finalized . 2. Input The triage engine reads all structured probing answers stored against the in-progress SR (e.g., issue type, vehicle drivable Yes/No, accident severity, safety status). 3. Rule Evaluation A configurable rules engine evaluates the answers against a defined decision matrix: for ex: Vehicle won't start / mechanical failure → Towing Flat tyre / dead battery / minor mechanical → ROS Accident with vehicle drivable → ROS Accident with vehicle not drivable → Towing Customer unsure / ambiguous → flagged for L2 manual decision 4. Classification &amp; Tagging System tags the SR with the classification ( case type = Towing | ROS | Manual Review ) and the rule(s) that drove the decision — so L2 sees both the outcome and the reasoning. 5. Routing Towing cases → routed to driver pool in L2 queue. ROS cases → routed to technician pool in L2 queue. Manual Review cases → routed to L2 with a flag for agent decision. 6. Override L2 agents can override the classification with a mandatory reason — captured for audit and used to refine the rules over time. Exception Handling If probing answers are incomplete → SR is created but flagged as Manual Review . If the rules engine fails or times out → SR defaults to Manual_Review (no case is ever blocked). Every classification (and override) is logged against the SR with timestamp, rule fired, and decision-maker (system vs agent). Device Setup GPS device installed on each driver/technician vehicle, transmitting coordinates at a configured frequency (e.g., every 10–15 seconds). 2. Driver-Side Journey Control Driver taps Start Journey in the Buddy App when leaving for the incident. Driver taps Stop Journey on arrival. Status changes flow into the CRM in real time. 3. ETA Calculation Base ETA → calculated from live GPS distance and traffic data (e.g., 36 min). Buffered ETA → configurable buffer added on top (e.g., +10 min) producing the displayed ETA (e.g., 45–50 min). ETA recalculates continuously as the driver moves; updates pushed at configured intervals to avoid spam. 4. Where Data is Shown Agent CRM → live map, current location, distance to incident, buffered ETA, journey status (Not Started / In Transit / Arrived / Stopped). Customer WhatsApp → buffered ETA in vendor-assigned message and tracking link. Driver Buddy App → original (unbuffered) ETA so the driver targets the tighter time. 5. Stop &amp; Deviation Detection Driver stops mid-journey beyond threshold (e.g., 5 min) → case auto-flags into Stopped queue, ETA paused, agent alerted. Route deviation → case flagged for review and ETA recalculated. 6. SLA Linkage If buffered ETA exceeds SLA, case auto-flags into Approaching SLA queue for early intervention. 7. Tracking Visibility Agent can pull up the tracker via SR/ticket or vehicle number. TL, RNM, ZM, and Management get scoped views via role-based access. Exception Handling GPS signal lost → last known location and last calculated ETA shown with timestamp + "signal lost" indicator. Device offline → flagged in the agent's queue. Driver forgets to tap Start Journey → system auto-detects movement and prompts. Heavy deviation → ETA recalculates; alert pushed if SLA crossed. All location data, ETA events, journey events, and exceptions logged against the SR. Trigger Auto-assignment runs immediately after SR creation and triage classification (Towing or ROS). 2. Eligibility Filtering System builds the eligible resource pool based on: Case type (Towing → drivers, ROS → technicians). Availability — logged in, free, not on another case. Proximity — within configured radius of incident location. Resource type preference — COCO first, then ASP (or as configured). Skill / vehicle-type match where applicable. 3. Recommended List in L2 CRM L2 sees the recommended technician/driver list on the CRM before broadcast. Vendors displayed in ascending order of distance from incident, showing name, type (COCO/ASP), distance in KM, ETA, and availability status. Colour coding: Green → available now. Amber → busy, with expected free time = current ETA to their drop + configurable buffer (e.g., 15 min). Grey → not logged in. Red → flagged (suspended, low rating, etc.). Filters: fleet type, vendor type (COCO/ASP), skill, distance radius. 4. Broadcast (Bidding) Upon L2 approval (or auto-trigger where configured): System pushes the case as a bid notification to all eligible resources via the Buddy App. Notification shows incident location, distance from resource, and case type. Bidding window is configurable (e.g., 60 seconds). 5. Acceptance First qualified resource to tap Accept gets the case. Case is locked to that resource; all others receive a "case closed" notification. Assignment logged with timestamp, resource ID, and acceptance latency. 6. Bidding Failure → L2 Fallback If no resource accepts within the bidding window, or bidding is disabled for the case type: System surfaces a ranked recommendation list to L2 (distance, performance score, COCO preference weights). Agent can assign directly with one click, or skip to manual search. 7. ASP-over-COCO Reason Capture At the moment of assignment, system checks: Is the selected vendor an ASP? Is at least one COCO resource also available within the eligible radius? If both → reason-capture popup opens. Mandatory dropdown with predefined reasons: COCO declined verbally. COCO too far despite radius eligibility. COCO skill mismatch. Customer specifically requested ASP. Other (free text required). Plus an optional free-text comment field. Reason, agent ID, timestamp, available COCO list, and chosen ASP all logged against the SR. Rolls up to dashboards for network planning analysis. 8. Bidding-Disabled Cases Certain case types or geographies may be configured to skip bidding entirely and go straight to L2 with the ranked list — e.g., VIP customers, accident cases, high-value claims. 9. Reassignment If the assigned resource cancels or recalls the case, the system re-broadcasts (bidding) or re-surfaces the ranked list (L2 fallback), based on configuration. Exception Handling No eligible resource in the pool → case routed to L2 with No Match flag (with prompt to expand radius or escalate). Bidding window expires with no acceptance → automatic L2 fallback. Resource accepts but does not start journey within X minutes → auto-recall and re-broadcast. Vendor declines → fallback to next in list. Stale GPS data → flagged with timestamp. Agent tries to skip ASP-over-COCO popup → assignment blocked until reason captured. "Other" reason selected with no free text → save blocked until comment entered. Every bid notification, acceptance, decline, expiry, reassignment, and ASP-override event logged against the SR. The CRM must surface exceptions into pre-built queues and show a live SLA countdown on every case. Give every L2 agent a left-rail set of auto-populating queues and a live SLA countdown on every case — so agents work top-down by priority and intervene before breach. 1. Queue Layout Left-rail navigation in the CRM with queues such as: Unassigned — SRs not yet allocated. Outstanding — open cases the agent owns. Auto-Population Cases move in and out of queues automatically based on real-time status (GPS, SLA timer, driver action, agent action) — no manual tagging needed. 3. SLA Engine Each case type carries a defined SLA (e.g., ROS = 60 min, Towing = 90 min). SLA clock starts at SR creation or vendor assignment (configurable). Clock can pause on defined events (e.g., customer unreachable) and resume, with full audit of pause reasons and durations. 4. Live SLA Display On every case view and queue row: Time Elapsed — minutes since SLA clock started. Time Remaining — minutes left to SLA. Visual Indicator — colour-coded (Green &gt; 50% remaining, Amber 20–50%, Red &lt; 20%, Black after breach). 5. SLA-Driven Queue Movement Case auto-moves into Approaching SLA queue when in Amber. Case auto-moves into Breached queue when in Black. Triggers configurable alerts to agent + TL. 6. Case Interaction Each queue shows a live case count. Each row is clickable, showing SR#, customer, location, ETA, time-lapse, SLA indicator. Click → Full Case View Opens for Action (chat, call, reassign, recall). 7. Filters &amp; Sorting Within each queue, agents can filter by date range, fleet type, driver/technician, location, client. Sort by SLA proximity, time-lapse, or distance. 8. Reporting SLA performance rolls up to dashboards for TL, RNM, ZM, Management — per agent, per resource, per location, per client.
